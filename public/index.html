How can I tell if something is a scam?+

Watch for pressure to act quickly, requests for upfront fees, guaranteed returns, suspicious links, secrecy, and requests for passwords or verification codes. Verify claims through official channels you find independently.

Are questionnaire responses published immediately?+

No. Responses should remain pending until a moderator reviews them, removes identifying information, and approves a safe public version.

Is the questionnaire completely anonymous?+

The form does not ask for your name or email. However, free-text details or infrastructure logs can potentially identify someone. Do not share private details, and read the site's privacy notice before submitting.

Can the AI guarantee that a link or offer is safe?+

No. AI guidance can be incomplete or wrong. Treat it as educational support, verify independently, and use official reporting or support channels for high-risk situations.

What if I have already sent money or shared a password?+

Contact your bank or payment provider promptly through an official channel, change compromised passwords from a trusted device, enable multifactor authentication, secure related accounts, and preserve evidence. Beware of people promising to recover money for an upfront fee.

Why do some people continue scamming others?+

Reasons can include financial incentives, social pressure, rationalization, fear, or involvement in criminal networks. Understanding these factors does not excuse harm. Legitimate work and support are safer alternatives.

How can I request removal of a published story?+

Contact the site administrator through the official contact channel listed by the site owner. Avoid sending passwords, bank details, or unnecessary identity documents in a removal request.
